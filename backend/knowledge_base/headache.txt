## Overview
A headache is pain or discomfort felt in the head, scalp, or neck. Most headaches are not dangerous and settle on their own or with simple self-care. The two most common types are tension headaches, which feel like a dull, band-like pressure around the head, and migraines, which are often one-sided, throbbing, and can come with nausea or sensitivity to light and sound.

## Common Triggers
Headaches are frequently linked to stress, poor sleep, dehydration, skipping meals, eye strain, poor posture, caffeine withdrawal, and minor illnesses such as colds. Identifying and adjusting these everyday triggers is often more useful than reaching for medication every time.

## Self-Care Measures
Resting in a quiet, dim room, drinking water, applying a cold or warm compress, gentle neck stretches, and taking a short break from screens can all help with a typical tension headache. Over-the-counter pain relief can be used according to label instructions if self-care measures are not enough.

## When to Seek Urgent Medical Attention
A headache should be treated as a medical emergency if it is the "worst headache of your life," comes on suddenly and severely, or is accompanied by confusion, slurred speech, weakness on one side of the body, a stiff neck with fever, vision loss, or a head injury. Headaches that are new after age 50, that steadily worsen over days or weeks, or that wake a person from sleep also warrant prompt evaluation by a healthcare professional.
