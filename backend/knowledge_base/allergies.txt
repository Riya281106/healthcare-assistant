## Overview
An allergy occurs when the immune system reacts to a normally harmless substance, such as pollen, dust, certain foods, insect stings, or medications, as if it were a threat. Reactions range from mild and localized to severe and life-threatening.

## Common Symptoms
Mild to moderate allergic reactions can include sneezing, itchy or watery eyes, runny nose, skin rashes or hives, and mild swelling. Food or insect-sting allergies can additionally cause stomach upset, tingling in the mouth, or localized swelling near the exposure site.

## Self-Care Measures
Avoiding known triggers, using antihistamines as directed for mild symptoms, and keeping the environment free of common allergens (such as regular cleaning to reduce dust) can help manage everyday allergies. Anyone with a known severe allergy should carry prescribed emergency medication as advised by their doctor.

## When to Seek Emergency Care
Anaphylaxis is a severe, rapid-onset allergic reaction and a medical emergency. Warning signs include difficulty breathing or swallowing, swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat, a sudden drop in blood pressure, widespread hives with dizziness, or loss of consciousness. Emergency services should be contacted immediately, and any prescribed emergency medication (such as an epinephrine auto-injector) should be used without delay if available.
