## Overview
Good sleep is essential for physical health, mood, memory, and immune function. Most adults need roughly 7-9 hours of sleep per night, though individual needs vary. Poor sleep quality or insufficient sleep over time is linked to a wide range of health problems, including fatigue, difficulty concentrating, and increased risk of chronic disease.

## Common Sleep Disruptors
Irregular sleep schedules, excessive screen time before bed, caffeine or alcohol close to bedtime, stress, an uncomfortable sleep environment, and untreated conditions such as sleep apnea can all disrupt sleep quality.

## Healthy Sleep Habits
Keeping a consistent sleep and wake schedule, creating a dark and quiet sleep environment, limiting screens and caffeine in the hours before bed, getting regular daytime physical activity, and reserving the bed mainly for sleep can meaningfully improve sleep quality over time.

## When to Seek Medical Attention
Persistent insomnia lasting several weeks, loud snoring with pauses in breathing (a possible sign of sleep apnea), excessive daytime sleepiness affecting daily function, or sleep problems accompanied by low mood or anxiety are worth discussing with a healthcare professional rather than managing indefinitely with self-help measures alone.
