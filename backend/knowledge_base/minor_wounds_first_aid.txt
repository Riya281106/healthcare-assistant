TITLE: First Aid for Minor Cuts, Scrapes, and Burns

## Minor Cuts and Scrapes
Clean the wound gently with running water and mild soap, remove any visible dirt or debris, and apply gentle pressure with a clean cloth to stop minor bleeding. Once bleeding stops, apply an antibiotic ointment if available and cover with a clean bandage, changing it daily or whenever it becomes wet or dirty.

## Minor Burns
Cool a minor burn immediately under cool (not ice-cold) running water for about 10-20 minutes. Avoid ice, butter, or other home remedies directly on the burn. Once cooled, cover loosely with a clean, non-stick dressing. Do not pop any blisters that form, as this increases infection risk.

## Signs of Infection to Watch For
Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus, worsening pain, red streaking from the wound, or fever after an injury can indicate infection and should prompt medical evaluation.

## When to Seek Medical Attention
Seek medical care for a wound that is deep, gaping, or won't stop bleeding after 10 minutes of firm pressure, for burns larger than a few centimeters or affecting the face, hands, joints, or genitals, for any burn that looks white or charred, for wounds from a dirty or rusty object, for animal or human bites, or if the person is not up to date on tetanus vaccination.
