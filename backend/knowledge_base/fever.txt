## Overview
Fever is a temporary rise in body temperature, usually a sign that the body's immune system is responding to an infection. A normal body temperature is roughly 36.5-37.5°C (97.7-99.5°F); a reading at or above 38°C (100.4°F) is generally considered a fever in adults.

## Common Symptoms
Fever is often accompanied by chills, sweating, headache, muscle aches, weakness, loss of appetite, and general fatigue. These accompanying symptoms, rather than the exact number on a thermometer, are usually the more important thing to pay attention to.

## Self-Care Measures
Rest, drinking plenty of fluids, wearing light clothing, and keeping the room at a comfortable temperature can help the body manage a mild fever. Over-the-counter fever reducers may be used according to label instructions if the fever is causing significant discomfort.

## When to Seek Medical Attention
Adults should seek medical care for a fever above 39.4°C (103°F), a fever lasting more than three days, or fever accompanied by a severe headache, stiff neck, confusion, difficulty breathing, chest pain, persistent vomiting, a rash, or signs of dehydration. People with weakened immune systems, chronic illness, or who are pregnant should have a lower threshold for contacting a healthcare professional about any fever.
