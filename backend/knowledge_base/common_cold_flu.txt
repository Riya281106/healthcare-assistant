## Overview
The common cold and influenza are both viral respiratory infections, but they differ in severity. A cold usually develops gradually with a runny or blocked nose, sneezing, sore throat, and a mild cough. Flu tends to come on suddenly with high fever, body aches, chills, and marked fatigue, and generally feels more severe than a cold.

## Self-Care Measures
Rest, staying well hydrated, warm fluids such as tea or broth, saltwater gargles for a sore throat, and steam inhalation for congestion are common comfort measures. Over-the-counter cold and flu remedies can ease individual symptoms but do not shorten how long the illness lasts.

## Preventing Spread
Frequent handwashing, covering coughs and sneezes, avoiding close contact with others while symptomatic, and staying home when unwell all reduce the chance of spreading these common respiratory viruses to others.

## When to Seek Medical Attention
Most colds and flu resolve within one to two weeks. Medical attention is warranted for difficulty breathing or shortness of breath, chest pain or pressure, persistent high fever, symptoms that improve then suddenly worsen, confusion, or inability to keep fluids down. Older adults, young children, pregnant people, and those with underlying health conditions such as asthma, diabetes, or heart disease should seek care earlier given a higher risk of complications.
